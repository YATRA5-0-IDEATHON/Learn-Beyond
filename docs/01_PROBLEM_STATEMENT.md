# 01 — Problem Statement

## Theme: Quality Education (UN SDG 4)

## The Core Problem

Nepal's education system produces graduates who are **theoretically educated but practically unemployable**. Students spend 12–16 years in school and college but graduate without the real-life skills employers need. The result is a massive, growing gap between education and employment.

---

## The 5 Real Problems We Identified

### Problem 1 — Theory Without Practice
Nepal's curriculum is almost entirely theoretical. Students memorize textbooks, pass exams, and graduate — but have never done the actual work their degree claims they can do. A BBS graduate has never prepared a real financial report. A BSc CSIT graduate has never built and deployed a real application. The knowledge exists in their head but has never been applied to real work.

**Evidence:** Nepal's graduate unemployment rate is among the highest in South Asia. Employers consistently report that fresh graduates need 6–12 months of on-the-job training before they can contribute meaningfully.

### Problem 2 — No Access to Mentors or Professionals
In Kathmandu, students with connections can find internships or informal mentors. In Pokhara, Butwal, Dharan, or rural districts, there is almost no access to working professionals who can guide students. The quality of education a student receives is determined almost entirely by their geography and family connections — not their ability or effort.

**Evidence:** Most professional mentorship in Nepal happens through personal networks (family, friends, college seniors). Students without those networks have no equivalent path.

### Problem 3 — Credentials That Can't Be Trusted
Degrees and certificates in Nepal are widely distrusted by employers because they don't prove ability — they only prove attendance. Grade inflation, exam copying, and purchased certificates are common. Employers have no reliable way to identify genuinely capable candidates from a stack of identical-looking CVs.

**Evidence:** Most Nepali employers rely on personal references and informal interviews rather than academic credentials when hiring, because credentials alone are unreliable signals.

### Problem 4 — No Career Direction
Students choose their study field based on family pressure, social status, or what their friends are doing — not based on their interests, strengths, or the actual job market. A student who loves design ends up studying accounting because "it's safer." They graduate with a degree in something they don't enjoy and skills the market doesn't need in the form they were taught.

**Evidence:** Nepal's career counseling infrastructure is almost nonexistent outside elite private schools. Most students have never spoken to a professional in the field they're studying.

### Problem 5 — Rural Students Are Left Behind
Nepal has significant rural populations with limited internet connectivity, few qualified teachers, and almost no exposure to professional work environments. The quality education gap between Kathmandu and rural Nepal is enormous and growing. Digital learning platforms that require high bandwidth or assume urban infrastructure don't reach these students.

**Evidence:** Nepal's internet penetration outside major cities remains low. Rural schools often lack qualified teachers in technical subjects. Students in rural areas have almost no exposure to professional work environments.

---

## The Broken Triangle

Nepal's education ecosystem has three groups that should be connected but aren't:

```
        STUDENTS
       (want skills,
       need direction)
           |
           |  ← GAP →
           |
    PROFESSIONALS              EMPLOYERS
  (have real knowledge,    (need skilled workers,
   no scalable way          can't trust degrees,
   to share it)             no reliable pipeline)
```

- Students can't reach professionals.
- Professionals have no scalable way to mentor students.
- Employers can't identify capable candidates.
- Everyone loses.

---

## What Quality Education Actually Means

Quality education is not about more textbooks or better exam scores. According to UN SDG 4, quality education means:

> "Relevant and effective learning outcomes" and "skills for employment, decent work, and entrepreneurship."

By that definition, Nepal's education system is failing. Students are learning inputs (time, memorization) but not producing outputs (real skills, job readiness).

**MentorDo's definition of quality education:** A student has received quality education when they can demonstrate real professional skills through real work, verified by real professionals, and recognized by real employers.

## Who Is Affected

| Group | How they're affected |
|---|---|
| Students | Graduate without job-ready skills, can't find work, lose confidence |
| Fresh graduates | Unemployed or underemployed despite years of study |
| Rural students | Doubly disadvantaged — poor education AND no professional network |
| Employers | Can't find skilled candidates, waste time and money on training |
| Nepal's economy | Brain drain as skilled youth leave for opportunities abroad |
| Professionals | No scalable way to give back or find talent |
